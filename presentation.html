Mind-Flayer Patch v2

Adaptive Content Moderation Under Adversarial Constraints

Stranger Syntax

The Problem

  • A large-scale platform is under attack
  • Content causes real-world harm — not through lies, but through manipulation of context
  • Adversaries adapt in real time to detection strategies
  • We must build a system that is defensible, cost-aware, and adaptive

Emphasize that this isn’t about fake news detection — it’s about contextual manipulation.

The Constraints We Must Respect

Constraint What It Means
Signal Collapse Detection methods decay when overused
Partial Observability Content arrives in stages, not all at once
Human Trust Budget Only 20 human reviews/hour
Multilingual Ambiguity Urdu, Roman Urdu, English — context-dependent
Poisoned Feedback ~15% of human labels are noisy or adversarial
Budget Reality PKR 50,000/month (~$175 USD) for everything

Mid-Challenge Update

“A large portion of harmful content is factually correct, but contextually misleading.”

Fact-checking alone is not enough.

The system must reason about framing, narrative, and selective presentation.

Our Approach: 3-Stage Adaptive Pipeline

Stage 1: Keyword Scan

Fast, free, multilingual first filter

Multilingual keywords:

English: kill, bomb, attack, terrorism...
Urdu:    قتل، بم، حملہ، دہشت، نفرت
Roman:   maar, qatal, bomb, hamla...

Language detection:

if urdu_chars > 30%:
    return "urdu"
if roman_urdu_markers >= 2:
    return "roman_urdu"
return "english"

Cost: $0.00 | Latency: <50ms

Stage 2: Lightweight AI Analysis

Context-aware detection with prompt rotation

What it detects beyond keywords:

  • Selective facts presented to mislead
  • Cherry-picked statistics without context
  • Emotional manipulation with real data
  • True statements made misleading by omission
  • Misleading juxtaposition

Budget gate: If monthly budget exceeded, skip AI → escalate to human.

Model: Claude Haiku | Cost: ~$0.0002/call | Latency: ~2s

Stage 3: Deep Analysis + Human Escalation

Full-context reasoning for the hardest cases

Receives from Stage 2:

  • Original content + language
  • Stage 2 reasoning + verdict
  • Detected manipulation tactics

Analyzes:

  • Cultural/linguistic context
  • Satire vs. genuine threat
  • What context is missing

Critical principle:

“Content that is factually correct but deliberately omits important context IS harmful.”

If still uncertain → Escalate

Creates a ReviewItem with full AI reasoning for human moderator.

Model: Claude Haiku | Cost: ~$0.0004/call | Latency: ~4s

Constraint 1: Signal Collapse

“Every detection method becomes less effective if overused.”

Our solution: Detection method decay

  • Each prompt variant has a decay factor (1.0 → 0.1)
  • Every use reduces it by 2%
  • Selection is weighted random — overused methods picked less often
  • Minimum floor of 10% — never fully excluded
  • Manual reset available when needed

Constraint 2: Partial Observability

“Content arrives in stages — waiting for full data violates latency.”

Key: Stages 2 & 3 run as background tasks — the user gets an immediate response. Post status updates asynchronously.

Constraint 3: Human Trust Budget

“Only 20 human reviews per hour.”

Review queue provides:

  • Original post content
  • Full AI reasoning from Stage 2 + 3
  • Detected manipulation tactics
  • Approve / Reject with reviewer notes

Most content never needs a human. The 3-stage pipeline filters aggressively — only truly ambiguous cases escalate.

Constraint 4: Multilingual Ambiguity

“yeh fire hai” — praise or threat?

Language detection:

  • Urdu script (Unicode \u0600-\u06FF)
  • Roman Urdu markers: hai, nahi, yeh, kya, mein…
  • Fallback: English

Language passed to every AI prompt for context-aware analysis.

How we avoid hard classification:

  • Stage 2/3 prompts explicitly consider cultural context
  • “uncertain” is a valid verdict — no forced binary
  • Ambiguous content escalates to human reviewers who understand nuance

We penalize overconfidence, not uncertainty.

Constraint 5: Poisoned Feedback Loop

“~15% of human labels are noisy or adversarial.”

Our approach: Audit trail, not blind trust

  • Every human review is logged with reviewer ID, timestamp, and notes
  • AI reasoning is preserved alongside human decisions
  • No automatic retraining on raw labels
  • Discrepancies between AI and human can be audited
  • Foundation for future label confidence scoring

Constraint 6: Budget Reality

“PKR 50,000/month (~$175 USD) for everything.”

Estimated monthly cost:

Component Volume Cost
Stage 1 (keywords) Unlimited $0
Stage 2 (Haiku) ~50K calls ~$10
Stage 3 (Haiku) ~10K calls ~$4
Total ~$14/mo


$14 of $175 = 8% utilization

Room for 12x growth before budget pressure.

Design Reasoning: Why a Pipeline?

The core question: How should content flow through the system?

Alternatives we rejected:

  1. Single-model classification — Send everything to Claude
    • Costs ~$0.0004/post. At 100K posts/mo = $40. Feasible but wasteful — most content is obviously safe or obviously harmful
    • No adaptation. One prompt = one signal = easy for adversaries to learn
  2. Ensemble of models voting — Run 3 models, take majority
    • 3x cost for every post. Blows budget at scale
    • Adversary only needs to fool 2 of 3
  3. Human-first triage — Humans scan everything, AI assists
    • 20 reviews/hour = 480/day. Platform likely has thousands of posts. Impossible

Why staged pipeline wins:

  • Cost cascade: Most content exits at Stage 1 (free). Only uncertain cases pay for AI. Only the hardest cases use expensive Stage 3
  • Latency cascade: Easy decisions are instant. Hard decisions take longer — but that’s acceptable because they’re genuinely hard
  • Each stage adds different information: keywords → single AI perspective → full context reasoning
  • Maps directly to partial observability constraint: Stage 1 = metadata, Stage 2 = partial content, Stage 3 = full content

The pipeline is not just an optimization — it’s the natural shape of the problem.

Design Reasoning: Why These Thresholds?

Stage 1: Why 3+ keywords = auto-remove?

The tradeoff:

  • Too low (1 hit): “The bomb pasta recipe was killer” gets removed. High false positives → users lose trust in the platform
  • Too high (5+ hits): Genuinely harmful content with 3-4 harmful terms slips through to Stage 2, wasting AI budget
  • 3 hits: A post with 3 harmful keywords across 3 languages is almost certainly not accidental. False positive rate is low enough to justify auto-action

Why 0 hits = safe (skip AI)?

If a post contains zero matches across 24+ keywords in 3 languages, the probability it’s harmful via explicit content is very low. Contextual manipulation (no keywords) is harder to detect at Stage 1 — but that’s exactly what Stage 2’s prompt rotation handles.

The honest gap:

Contextually misleading content with zero harmful keywords will bypass Stage 1. This is by design.

  • “Unemployment rose 50% in Karachi” (true but cherry-picked from a recovery quarter)
  • Contains no harmful keywords
  • Passes Stage 1 as “safe”

Our mitigation: This is why the mid-challenge update matters. We added 5 manipulation-focused prompt variants specifically for content that is “factually correct but contextually misleading.” But these only run in Stage 2 — and Stage 2 only runs on uncertain content from Stage 1.

We accept this gap because the alternative (running AI on every post) costs 12x more and doesn’t respect the budget constraint.

Design Reasoning: Why Prompt Rotation?

The problem: If we use one prompt, adversaries learn exactly what it looks for and craft content to avoid it.

Key insight: Each prompt variant targets a different manipulation technique. An adversary who learns to bypass “explicit harm detection” still gets caught by “selective presentation detection.”

Design Reasoning: Why Decay, Not Rotation?

Alternative considered: Simple round-robin (cycle through prompts 1→2→3→4→5→1…)

Why round-robin fails:

  • Predictable. Adversary observes which posts get caught and when. After 5 posts, they know the cycle
  • No memory. If prompt 3 catches 90% of current attacks, round-robin still only uses it 20% of the time
  • No degradation signal. No way to know when a method is becoming ineffective

Why weighted random with decay wins:

  • Unpredictable. Even with identical content, the selected prompt varies
  • Self-adjusting. Overused methods naturally get selected less. Fresh methods get more weight
  • Observable. Dashboard shows decay factors — operators can see system health in real-time

The decay math:

  • decay_rate = 0.02 (2% per use)
  • min_decay = 0.1 (10% floor)
  • After 45 uses: 1.0 - (45 × 0.02) = 0.10 (floor)

Why 2% and not 5% or 10%?

  • 5-10%: Methods hit floor after 10-18 uses. Too aggressive — methods rotate too fast, the system becomes unstable
  • 1%: Takes 90 uses to reach floor. Too slow — adversary has too many attempts against the same method
  • 2%: ~45 uses per method. With 5 methods at equal usage, that’s ~225 total posts before any method hits floor. Reasonable for a daily cycle

Why a floor of 10%, not 0%?

Excluding a method entirely means adversaries know it’s gone. A 10% chance means it could still appear — maintaining uncertainty.

Design Reasoning: Budget Strategy

The budget is the hardest constraint. $175/mo sounds generous until you do the math.

Why Claude Haiku (not Sonnet/Opus)?

Model Input/1K tok Output/1K tok Cost per call
Haiku $0.00025 $0.00125 ~$0.0002
Sonnet $0.003 $0.015 ~$0.003
Opus $0.015 $0.075 ~$0.015
  • Sonnet = 15x more expensive → only ~58K calls/mo
  • Opus = 75x more expensive → only ~11K calls/mo
  • Haiku = ~875K calls/mo budget capacity

Tradeoff: Haiku is less capable at nuanced reasoning. We compensate with specific, targeted prompts that tell it exactly what to look for, rather than asking for general analysis.

Why not fine-tune a local model?

  • Fine-tuning cost: $500+ for a useful model. Exceeds 3 months of budget
  • Hosting cost: GPU instance ~$100-500/mo. Eats entire budget
  • Maintenance: Retraining as adversaries adapt requires ongoing compute
  • With Haiku at ~$14/mo, we have $161 of headroom for spikes

Graceful degradation when budget runs out:

Budget OK    → Stage 1 + 2 + 3 (full pipeline)
Budget tight → Stage 1 + 2 only (skip Stage 3)
Budget gone  → Stage 1 only (keywords + human)

The system never fully stops. It degrades to a less capable but still functional mode. Keywords still catch obvious harm. Humans still review escalated content.

Design Reasoning: Human Review Budget

20 reviews/hour = the system’s most precious resource.

Why enforce at submission time, not at queue entry?

  • Queue entry enforcement: Posts pile up in queue, moderator sees them but can’t act. Frustrating UX, false sense of backlog
  • Submission enforcement: Moderator sees exactly what they can act on. No phantom queue. HTTP 429 is clear feedback: “come back next hour”

Why not prioritize which posts humans see?

We considered scoring escalated posts by confidence and showing lowest-confidence first. We rejected this because:

  • Adds complexity without clear benefit at 20/hr
  • Low-confidence posts aren’t necessarily higher impact
  • FIFO is transparent — moderators trust it

How AI reasoning helps humans:

Each review item shows:

  • The AI’s verdict from Stage 2 and 3
  • The AI’s reasoning (natural language explanation)
  • Detected manipulation tactics (cherry-picked stats, loaded language, etc.)

This is deliberate. A moderator reviewing “yeh fire hai” without context is guessing. A moderator reviewing it with “AI detected possible threat language, but confidence is low because ‘fire’ is commonly used as praise in Roman Urdu” makes a better decision faster.

The tradeoff: AI reasoning could bias the human. We accept this because:

  • Unbiased humans with no context make worse decisions
  • The 15% noise rate already means human labels aren’t pure
  • AI reasoning is logged alongside human decisions for auditing

Design Reasoning: Multilingual Approach

Why heuristic language detection, not a library?

Alternatives considered:

  1. langdetect / fasttext: Good for clean text. Fails on Roman Urdu (looks like misspelled English), code-mixed text (“yeh so cool hai”), and short posts
  2. Google Translate API: Costs money per call. Adds latency. Eats into $175 budget
  3. Let Claude detect language: Already paying for AI in Stage 2. But Stage 1 needs language for keyword selection before Claude runs

Our heuristic:

# Urdu script = Urdu (high confidence)
if urdu_chars > 30% of text → "urdu"

# Roman Urdu markers (hai, nahi, yeh, kya, mein...)
if roman_urdu_words >= 2 → "roman_urdu"

# Fallback
else → "english"

Tradeoffs we accept:

  • False positive: “Yeh brand hai best” detected as Roman Urdu. Harmless — Stage 2 prompt includes language context either way
  • False negative: Single Roman Urdu word in English text (“that’s a jugaar solution”) detected as English. Low risk — Claude understands code-mixing regardless
  • 30% threshold for Urdu: Lower catches more Urdu but misclassifies Arabic. Higher misses short Urdu posts. 30% is a pragmatic middle ground

Why this matters for moderation:

“yeh fire hai” classified correctly as Roman Urdu triggers cultural context analysis in Stage 2/3 prompts. Misclassified as English, Claude might miss that “fire” is common praise in South Asian slang.

We don’t force binary classification. “Uncertain” language → AI gets the full text and figures it out.

Design Reasoning: Poisoned Labels

15% noise means 1 in ~7 human labels is wrong. This is a real threat.

What we do:

  • Store everything. Every human decision + AI reasoning + timestamps + reviewer identity
  • Never auto-retrain. Human labels do not flow back into the system’s detection logic
  • Preserve AI reasoning alongside human decisions. If AI said “harmful” and human said “safe,” that discrepancy is visible

Why this is enough for now:

The problem statement says “blindly trusting labels can reinforce harmful patterns.” Our system doesn’t trust labels at all — it uses them for final post disposition only, not for learning.

What we’d add with more time:

Per-reviewer trust scoring. Track each reviewer’s agreement rate with AI. Reviewers who consistently disagree with high-confidence AI verdicts get flagged. This catches coordinated adversarial labeling.

Design Reasoning: What We Chose NOT to Build

Simplicity with strong reasoning is valued. Here’s what we deliberately left out.

No ML training pipeline

  • Fine-tuning requires labeled data. Our labels have 15% noise
  • Training costs exceed monthly budget
  • A pre-trained LLM with good prompts outperforms a small fine-tuned model on nuanced tasks like context manipulation
  • Decision: Use Claude’s general reasoning ability + specific prompts

No image/video analysis

  • Problem statement focuses on text content and framing
  • Image analysis (OCR, deepfake detection) adds cost and complexity
  • Haiku’s vision capability exists but costs more per call
  • Decision: Text-only pipeline. Acknowledge gap in failure modes

No real-time adversary fingerprinting

  • Tracking posting patterns (timing, accounts, networks) is powerful but requires significant infrastructure
  • Rate limiting and user-level analysis need Redis, background workers, graph databases
  • Decision: Out of scope for budget. Acknowledge as future work

No Celery / Redis / message queue

  • FastAPI BackgroundTasks is simpler and sufficient
  • No persistent queue means if server restarts, in-flight moderation is lost
  • Tradeoff: Simplicity over durability. For a demo, this is fine. For production, we’d add Redis

No confidence calibration

  • Claude returns confidence scores (0.0-1.0) but they’re not calibrated
  • A “0.7 confidence” doesn’t mean 70% accuracy
  • Tradeoff: We use confidence for logging/display, not for hard decisions. Verdicts (safe/harmful/uncertain) drive logic, not scores

No A/B testing of prompts

  • We could run two prompts on the same content and compare
  • 2x cost per post. Not justified given budget headroom
  • Decision: Rely on decay mechanism to naturally rotate. Add A/B testing if detection quality drops

The Tradeoff Map

Every design decision is a tradeoff. Here’s how they connect:

Our position: We optimize for cost and adaptability first, accepting slightly lower accuracy on edge cases. We compensate with human review for the cases that matter most.

Failure Modes

We declare three scenarios where the system will fail:

1. Coordinated Novel Attack

A new manipulation tactic not covered by any prompt variant.

Impact: Harmful content passes through all 3 stages undetected.

Containment: Human reviewers catch patterns → new prompt variants added manually.

2. Budget Exhaustion Under Attack

Adversary floods system to burn through AI budget.

Impact: Falls back to keyword-only detection — many harmful posts slip through.

Containment: Rate limiting, keyword filter still catches obvious harm, escalation to humans continues.

3. Review Queue Saturation

More than 20 ambiguous posts/hour during a crisis.

Impact: Escalated posts wait in queue — delayed moderation.

Containment: AI still provides reasoning, most harmful content caught in Stages 1-2. Queue overflow is visible on dashboard.

Why This Works

  1. Simple reasoning over complex engineering — 3 clear stages, not a black box
  2. Adaptive by design — prompt rotation + decay prevents adversary learning
  3. Cost-efficient — $14/mo of $175 budget, with graceful degradation
  4. Honest about failure — declared failure modes with containment strategies
  5. Context over classification — detects manipulation, not just keywords
  6. Human-in-the-loop, not human-dependent — 20/hr budget used only when needed

“The enemy learns. Static systems fail. Only adaptive thinking survives.”
