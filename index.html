Mind-Flayer Patch v2

Adaptive Content Moderation Under Adversarial Constraints

Stranger Syntax

The Problem

  • A large-scale platform is under attack
  • Content causes real-world harm — not through lies, but through manipulation of context
  • Adversaries adapt in real time to detection strategies
  • We must build a system that is defensible, cost-aware, and adaptive

Emphasize that this isn’t about fake news detection — it’s about contextual manipulation.

The Constraints We Must Respect

Constraint What It Means
Signal Collapse Detection methods decay when overused
Partial Observability Content arrives in stages, not all at once
Human Trust Budget Only 20 human reviews/hour
Multilingual Ambiguity Urdu, Roman Urdu, English — context-dependent
Poisoned Feedback ~15% of human labels are noisy or adversarial
Budget Reality PKR 50,000/month (~$175 USD) for everything

Mid-Challenge Update

“A large portion of harmful content is factually correct, but contextually misleading.”

Fact-checking alone is not enough.

The system must reason about framing, narrative, and selective presentation.

Our Approach: 3-Stage Adaptive Pipeline

Stage 1: Keyword Scan

Multilingual keywords:

English: kill, bomb, attack, terrorism...
Urdu:    قتل، بم، حملہ، دہشت، نفرت
Roman:   maar, qatal, bomb, hamla...

Language detection:

if urdu_chars > 30%:       return "urdu"
if roman_urdu_markers >= 2: return "roman_urdu"
return "english"

Cost: $0.00 | Latency: <50ms

Stage 2: Lightweight AI Analysis

5 rotating prompt variants:

  1. safety_check_v1 — Explicit harm
  2. context_analyzer_v1 — Framing bias
  3. narrative_detector_v1 — Cherry-picked data
  4. framing_analyzer_v1 — Loaded language
  5. selective_presentation_v1 — Missing context

Detects beyond keywords: selective facts, cherry-picked stats, emotional manipulation, misleading omission

Cost: ~$0.0002/call | Latency: ~2s

Stage 3: Deep Analysis + Human Escalation

Receives from Stage 2:

  • Original content + detected language
  • Stage 2 reasoning + verdict
  • Detected manipulation tactics

Analyzes:

  • Cultural/linguistic context
  • Satire vs. genuine threat
  • What context is missing

Critical principle:

“Content that is factually correct but deliberately omits important context IS harmful.”

If still uncertain → Escalate

Creates a ReviewItem with full AI reasoning for the human moderator.

Cost: ~$0.0004/call | Latency: ~4s

Constraint 1: Signal Collapse

“Every detection method becomes less effective if overused.”

Our solution: Detection method decay

  • Each prompt has a decay factor (1.0 → 0.1)
  • Every use reduces it by 2%
  • Selection is weighted random
  • Floor of 10% — never fully excluded
  • Manual reset when needed

Constraint 2: Partial Observability

“Content arrives in stages — waiting for full data violates latency.”

Stages 2 & 3 run as background tasks — user gets an immediate response. Post status updates asynchronously.

Constraint 3: Human Trust Budget

“Only 20 human reviews per hour.”

Review queue provides:

  • Original post content
  • Full AI reasoning from Stage 2 + 3
  • Detected manipulation tactics
  • Approve / Reject with reviewer notes

Most content never needs a human. The 3-stage pipeline filters aggressively — only truly ambiguous cases escalate.

Constraint 4: Multilingual Ambiguity

“yeh fire hai” — praise or threat?

Language detection:

  • Urdu script (Unicode \u0600-\u06FF)
  • Roman Urdu markers: hai, nahi, yeh, kya, mein…
  • Fallback: English

Language passed to every AI prompt for context-aware analysis.

How we avoid hard classification:

  • Stage 2/3 prompts explicitly consider cultural context
  • “uncertain” is a valid verdict — no forced binary
  • Ambiguous content escalates to human reviewers who understand nuance

We penalize overconfidence, not uncertainty.

Constraint 5: Poisoned Feedback Loop

“~15% of human labels are noisy or adversarial.”

Our approach: Audit trail, not blind trust

  • Every review logged with reviewer ID, timestamp, notes
  • AI reasoning preserved alongside human decisions
  • No automatic retraining on raw labels
  • Discrepancies between AI and human can be audited
  • Foundation for future label confidence scoring

Constraint 6: Budget Reality

“PKR 50,000/month (~$175 USD) for everything.”

Estimated monthly cost:

Component Volume Cost
Stage 1 (keywords) Unlimited $0
Stage 2 (Haiku) ~50K calls ~$10
Stage 3 (Haiku) ~10K calls ~$4
Total ~$14/mo

$14 of $175 = 8% utilization — room for 12x growth.

Reasoning: Why a Pipeline?

Alternatives we rejected:

1. Single-model classification — Send everything to Claude

  • ~$0.0004/post × 100K = $40/mo. Feasible but wasteful
  • One prompt = one signal = easy for adversaries to learn

2. Ensemble voting — 3 models, majority wins

  • 3x cost for every post. Blows budget at scale
  • Adversary only needs to fool 2 of 3

3. Human-first triage

  • 20/hr = 480/day. Platform has thousands of posts. Impossible

Why staged pipeline wins:

  • Cost cascade: Most content exits at Stage 1 (free). Only uncertain cases pay for AI
  • Latency cascade: Easy decisions are instant. Hard decisions take longer — acceptable
  • Each stage adds different info: keywords → AI perspective → full context
  • Maps to partial observability: Stage 1 = metadata, Stage 2 = partial, Stage 3 = full

The pipeline is not just an optimization — it’s the natural shape of the problem.

Reasoning: Why These Thresholds?

Stage 1: Why 3+ keywords = auto-remove?

The tradeoff:

  • Too low (1 hit): “The bomb pasta recipe was killer” gets removed. Users lose trust
  • Too high (5+): Harmful content with 3-4 terms slips to Stage 2, wasting AI budget
  • 3 hits: Across 3 languages, almost certainly not accidental

Why 0 hits = safe (skip AI)?

Zero matches across 24+ keywords in 3 languages → probability of explicit harm is very low.

The honest gap we accept:

Contextually misleading content with zero harmful keywords will bypass Stage 1.

  • “Unemployment rose 50% in Karachi” (true but cherry-picked)
  • Contains no harmful keywords → passes as “safe”

Mitigation: 5 manipulation-focused prompt variants in Stage 2 handle “factually correct but misleading” content.

We accept this gap because running AI on every post costs 12x more.

Reasoning: Why Prompt Rotation?

If we use one prompt, adversaries learn what it looks for.

Each variant targets a different manipulation technique. Bypassing “explicit harm detection” doesn’t help against “selective presentation detection.”

Reasoning: Why Decay, Not Round-Robin?

Why round-robin fails:

  • Predictable. After 5 posts, adversary knows the cycle
  • No memory. If prompt 3 catches 90% of attacks, it still only runs 20% of the time
  • No degradation signal. No way to know when a method is failing

Why weighted random + decay wins:

  • Unpredictable. Same content, different prompt each time
  • Self-adjusting. Overused methods naturally selected less
  • Observable. Dashboard shows decay — operators see health

The decay math:

  • decay_rate = 0.02 (2% per use)
  • min_decay = 0.1 (10% floor)
  • After 45 uses: reaches floor

Why 2% specifically?

  • 5-10%: Floor after 10-18 uses. Too aggressive — system becomes unstable
  • 1%: 90 uses to floor. Too slow — adversary exploits same method too long
  • 2%: ~45 uses/method, ~225 total posts before any hits floor. Good daily cycle

Why floor at 10%, not 0%?

Excluding a method entirely means adversaries know it’s gone. 10% keeps them uncertain.

Reasoning: Budget Strategy

Why Claude Haiku, not Sonnet or Opus?

Model Cost/call Monthly capacity
Haiku ~$0.0002 ~875K calls
Sonnet ~$0.003 ~58K calls
Opus ~$0.015 ~11K calls

Haiku is 15x cheaper than Sonnet.

Tradeoff: Less capable at nuanced reasoning. We compensate with specific, targeted prompts that tell it exactly what to look for.

Why not fine-tune a local model?

  • Fine-tuning: $500+. Exceeds 3 months of budget
  • GPU hosting: $100-500/mo. Eats entire budget
  • Retraining as adversaries adapt = ongoing cost
  • Labels have 15% noise — fine-tuning on bad data

Graceful degradation:

Budget OK    → Full pipeline (S1+S2+S3)
Budget tight → S1 + S2 only
Budget gone  → S1 only (keywords + human)

System never fully stops — degrades gracefully.

Reasoning: Human Review Budget

20 reviews/hour = the system’s most precious resource.

Why enforce at submission, not queue entry?

  • Queue entry: Posts pile up, moderator sees but can’t act. Frustrating UX
  • Submission: Moderator sees exactly what they can act on. HTTP 429 = clear “come back next hour”

Why not prioritize by confidence?

  • Low-confidence posts aren’t necessarily higher impact
  • FIFO is transparent — moderators trust it
  • Adds complexity without clear benefit at 20/hr

How AI reasoning helps humans:

Each review item shows AI verdict, reasoning, and manipulation tactics.

A moderator reviewing “yeh fire hai” with “possible threat, but ‘fire’ is common praise in Roman Urdu” makes a better decision faster.

Tradeoff: AI reasoning could bias the human.

We accept this because unbiased humans with no context make worse decisions, and AI reasoning is logged for auditing.

Reasoning: Multilingual Approach

Why heuristic language detection, not a library?

Alternatives considered:

  1. langdetect / fasttext — Fails on Roman Urdu (looks like misspelled English), code-mixed text, short posts
  2. Google Translate API — Costs money, adds latency, eats budget
  3. Let Claude detect — Stage 1 needs language before Claude runs

Our heuristic:

if urdu_chars > 30%:       → "urdu"
if roman_urdu_words >= 2:  → "roman_urdu"
else:                      → "english"

Tradeoffs we accept:

  • False positive: “Yeh brand hai best” → Roman Urdu. Harmless — Claude handles it fine
  • False negative: Single Roman Urdu word in English missed. Low risk — Claude understands code-mixing
  • 30% Urdu threshold: Lower misclassifies Arabic. Higher misses short Urdu. Pragmatic middle

Why it matters:

“yeh fire hai” classified as Roman Urdu triggers cultural context analysis. Misclassified as English, Claude might miss that “fire” = common South Asian praise.

Reasoning: Poisoned Labels

15% noise = 1 in ~7 labels is wrong.

What we do:

  • Store everything. Decision + AI reasoning + timestamps + reviewer ID
  • Never auto-retrain. Labels don’t flow back into detection logic
  • Preserve AI reasoning alongside human decisions. Discrepancies are visible

Why this is enough:

Problem says “blindly trusting labels reinforces harm.” We don’t trust labels at all — used for post disposition only, not learning.

What we’d add with more time:

Per-reviewer trust scoring catches coordinated adversarial labeling.

Reasoning: What We Chose NOT to Build

No ML training pipeline

  • Labels have 15% noise. Fine-tuning cost > budget
  • LLM + specific prompts outperforms small fine-tuned models on nuanced tasks

No image/video analysis

  • Problem focuses on text + framing
  • Vision adds cost; text-only pipeline acknowledged as a gap

No adversary fingerprinting

  • Tracking posting patterns needs Redis, graph DBs
  • Out of scope for budget

No Celery / Redis

  • FastAPI BackgroundTasks is sufficient for demo
  • Tradeoff: Server restart loses in-flight work

No confidence calibration

  • Claude’s 0.7 confidence ≠ 70% accuracy
  • Verdicts (safe/harmful/uncertain) drive logic, not scores

No A/B testing of prompts

  • 2x cost per post, not justified
  • Decay mechanism naturally rotates instead

The Tradeoff Map

Our position: Optimize for cost and adaptability first, accept slightly lower accuracy on edge cases. Human review compensates for the hardest cases.

Failure Modes

We declare three scenarios where the system will fail.

No system is perfect — we are evaluated on honesty about limitations and how damage is contained.

  1. Coordinated Novel Attack — a manipulation tactic we’ve never seen
  2. Budget Exhaustion Under Attack — adversary burns our AI budget
  3. Review Queue Saturation — more escalations than humans can handle

Failure 1: Coordinated Novel Attack

What happens:

A new manipulation technique (e.g. sarcasm-based radicalization, meme-text hybrid) that none of our 5 prompt variants are designed to detect.

Impact: Harmful content passes all 3 stages undetected. Users are exposed until the gap is noticed.

How we detect it: Human reviewers and user reports surface patterns that AI missed. Dashboard shows sudden drop in “harmful” verdicts — a signal that something is slipping through.

Overcoming Failure 1

Short-term containment:

  • Human reviewers catch novel patterns during normal queue work
  • Manual escalation button lets moderators flag suspicious “safe” content
  • User reports surface content the pipeline missed

Long-term fix:

  • Write a new prompt variant targeting the novel tactic
  • Add it to detection_methods table with decay_factor = 1.0
  • Fresh method gets high selection weight immediately
  • Existing methods are not disrupted — pool simply grows

Why this works: The system is designed for prompt extensibility. Adding a 6th or 7th variant requires no code changes — just a database insert and a new prompt template.

Failure 2: Budget Exhaustion Under Attack

What happens:

Adversary submits thousands of posts designed to trigger Stage 2 (1-2 keyword hits each). Each call costs ~$0.0002. At 875K calls the budget is gone.

Impact: System falls back to keyword-only mode. Contextual manipulation (the hardest content) goes undetected. Only obvious keyword-heavy harm is caught.

How we detect it: Budget dashboard shows spend spiking. budget_utilization_pct jumps from normal 8% toward 100%. Alert is visible in System Health page.

Overcoming Failure 2

Short-term containment:

  • Budget gate already exists — Stage 2/3 skip gracefully when budget is exhausted
  • Keywords still catch obvious harm (free, unlimited)
  • Human escalation still works (posts marked “uncertain” go to queue)
  • System never fully stops — it degrades

Long-term fix:

  • Per-user rate limiting — cap posts/hour per account. Flood attacks require many accounts
  • Tiered AI usage — new/low-trust accounts get keyword-only. Established accounts get full pipeline
  • Adaptive budget allocation — reserve 20% of budget as emergency buffer, only used for high-signal content
  • Cost-aware routing — if budget is at 80%, skip Stage 3 and escalate uncertain Stage 2 content directly to humans

Failure 3: Review Queue Saturation

What happens:

A real-world crisis (election, natural disaster, conflict) generates a surge of ambiguous, context-dependent content. AI can’t confidently classify it → mass escalation to humans.

Impact: Posts queue up beyond 20/hour capacity. Harmful content stays visible while waiting for review. Moderation is delayed by hours during the most critical period.

How we detect it: Review queue length grows visibly on dashboard. remaining in budget endpoint drops to 0. HTTP 429 responses increase.

Overcoming Failure 3

Short-term containment:

  • AI reasoning is already attached to every queued post — moderators can work faster with context
  • Most harmful content is caught in Stage 1-2 before reaching the queue
  • Queue overflow is visible on dashboard — operators can respond

Long-term fix:

  • Auto-hold policy — during crisis, uncertain content is hidden from the feed until reviewed (safe default)
  • Priority scoring — rank queue by content reach (followers, shares). High-reach posts reviewed first
  • Emergency budget increase — temporarily raise 20/hr cap during declared crises with additional moderators
  • Confidence-based auto-action — if Stage 3 confidence > 0.9, auto-act instead of escalating. Only truly ambiguous cases (confidence 0.4-0.7) reach humans

Tech Stack

Everything runs with: make up + make dev-frontend

Why This Works

  1. Simple reasoning over complex engineering — 3 clear stages, not a black box
  2. Adaptive by design — prompt rotation + decay prevents adversary learning
  3. Cost-efficient — $14/mo of $175 budget, with graceful degradation
  4. Honest about failure — declared failure modes with containment
  5. Context over classification — detects manipulation, not just keywords
  6. Human-in-the-loop, not human-dependent — 20/hr used only when needed

“The enemy learns. Static systems fail. Only adaptive thinking survives.”
